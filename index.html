From the Vice Chancellor

“We teach the people who are already carrying something.”

Most of the students who walk through our gate at Mengo are not coming straight from a classroom. They are coming from a job, a business, a ward, a farm, a family. They have something to carry, and they have decided to carry a qualification as well.

That is the university we have built for them. Our lectures run in the evening and at the weekend as readily as they run by day, because a person should not have to choose between earning and learning. Our programmes — 99 of them, from national certificates in the trades to masters degrees — are set to be finished by people who are busy, and to be worth something to an employer at the end.

You will be taught by staff who know your field and who will know your name. Come and see us, or apply online, and let us talk about where you want to be in three years.

Prof. Lutalo Bbosa Vice Chancellor, Team University

More about the university
Study modes

Built for people who are already busy.

Most of our students are working, raising families, or both. Every faculty runs more than one schedule so that a job is not a reason to stop studying.

Day

Weekday classes for full-time students straight from A-level or a diploma.

Evening

After-work classes in town, for people holding down a job.

Weekend

Friday to Sunday, so a working week stays intact.

Distance

Study from anywhere in Uganda, with materials and support online.

Campus life

Mengo, Kampala — five minutes from the city.

Student life
Why Team

What you get, beyond the certificate.

Recognised awards

Degrees and diplomas awarded by Team University, and vocational awards assessed by the national boards — so what you earn is registered where it counts.

A student portal that works

Registration, results, transcripts and fees statements online, with your own login from the day you are admitted.

A schedule that fits your job

Day, evening, weekend and distance — and you can ask to change mode between semesters if your work changes.

In the middle of Kampala

Wood House Mengo, on Kabaka A'njagala Road — reachable from anywhere in the city, and close to where our students already live and work.
